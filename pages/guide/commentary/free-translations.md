# Free translations

`\ft` gives an [IGT](#guide-writing-a-text) its free translation: what the whole line means, in ordinary words, rather than word by word.

```gloss "Indonesian (Sneddon 1996: 237). Leipzig Glossing Rules, example 1."
\igt
\text Mereka di Jakarta sekarang.
\glmorph they in Jakarta now
\ft They are in Jakarta now.
```

- A translation can take several lines of the source, and keeps its line breaks, so a verse can be translated line by line.
- It can be formatted, and can have links (see [Formatting and links](#guide-formatting-and-links)).
- An IGT has one translation; a second `\ft` is flagged, and dropped.

```gloss
\igt 1.8
\text Mūsa, mihī causās memorā, quō nūmine laesō
\ft Muse, recount to me the causes:
    what divinity was offended,
```

## Where translations appear

In *Display options* (the *Commentary* group), the translation can be shown below each IGT, beside it, or not at all. Beside it, the translations make a column of their own next to the text (on the left, for a text written right to left), which goes below the text on a narrow screen.
